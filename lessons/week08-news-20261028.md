## Switch 2: homebrew, game mods and control

**22 and 26 October 2026.** Polygon reports a Switch 2 exploit used for
homebrew and game modifications, followed by expanded file-system access that
can copy game files to a computer. The reporting distinguishes that access
from full kernel control. The [original demo repository](https://gitlab.com/pepsipu/ns2jb)
describes a temporary, firmware-specific proof of concept; it is not a
universal or permanent custom firmware. We have not tested it on a console.

Sources: [initial report](https://www.polygon.com/nintendo-switch-2-ai-jailbreak-mario-kart-world/) ·
[file-system follow-up](https://www.polygon.com/nintendo-switch-2-hackers-jailbreak-dumping-files-modding/).

**Question:** If you could extend a closed platform, what new experience would
you make? Where is the difference between learning from a mechanic, remixing
with permission and redistributing someone else's work?

Keep it a discussion of homebrew, reverse engineering and platform access, not
an instruction to modify a student's console. Do not claim AI alone discovered
the exploit or that all Switch 2 systems can run it. Polygon describes the work
as AI-assisted; the upstream README acknowledges AI assistance in packaging.

## AI clones and showing work in progress

**19 October 2026.** [Ars Technica reports](https://arstechnica.com/gaming/2026/10/ai-game-clones-are-making-indie-developers-afraid-to-show-their-work/)
that Alliance Arts postponed a public demo of *Drunken Goddess Reflux*, citing
concerns that its presentation and gameplay could be copied before launch. This
is a stated concern, not evidence that this demo was actually cloned.

**Question:** What makes your group's game its own experience beyond a single
mechanic? How could you get useful playtest feedback while crediting sources
and respecting other creators' work?

enterprise license. This is a future change, not an already-free console route.

**27 October 2026:** [Game Developer reports](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/epic-is-updating-the-eula-to-allow-ai-training-on-outputs-from-unreal-engine-and-metahuman)

have their own terms. Treat it as a proposed change, not a rule already in
force; check the actual current agreement before drawing conclusions.
